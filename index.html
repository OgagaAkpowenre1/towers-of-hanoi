Towers of Hanoi

About the Game

History

The Towers of Hanoi is a mathematical puzzle invented by French mathematician Édouard Lucas in 1883. Legend says it's based on an ancient Indian temple where priests move 64 golden disks between three diamond needles.

How to Play

Click on a tower to select the top disk
Click on another tower to move the disk there
Only one disk can be moved at a time
Never place a larger disk on top of a smaller one

Objective

Move the entire stack of disks from the starting tower to any other tower, following the rules above. Try to complete it in the fewest moves possible!

The Math Behind It

The minimum number of moves required to solve the puzzle is given by the formula:

M = 2n - 1

Where n is the number of disks and M is the minimum moves required.

Fun Fact: With 64 disks, it would take 264-1 moves (18,446,744,073,709,551,615). At one move per second, that's over 584 billion years!

Strategy Tips

  • Always move the smallest disk first when possible
  • Try to create empty towers for temporary storage
  • Work on moving larger disks to their final positions early
  • The puzzle can be solved recursively - solve for n-1 disks, then move the largest
